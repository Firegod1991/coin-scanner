# Coin Scanner

A live overlay for a USB UVC camera. It locks onto a United States coin and marks what a webcam can actually see: rim bites, long lines, round pits, green spots, a design that sits off center, and parallel split edges.

It is a screening tool. It does not grade a coin, and it does not authenticate a doubled die from one picture.

## Run

Double-click **Coin Scanner** on the desktop or in the Start menu. That opens its own window, separate from the browser. The same address, http://127.0.0.1:8765, still works in a browser tab.

Opening the shortcut again brings the window back. It does not start a second camera. Quit inside the window stops the scanner.

This PC's USB2.0 UVC PC Camera is selected when it is present. The driver picture is 640x480. Asking that camera for 720p turns the picture black. If the picture is black, unplug the camera, plug it back in, and press Reconnect. Skip an infrared camera if one appears. The picture from an IR camera looks gray or purple.

## How to scan

1. Put the coin on a dark cloth.
2. Choose the series. The list covers regular United States issues from half cents through double eagles, including the 2026 semiquincentennial coins.
3. Fill the ring. USB 2.0 cameras are short on detail, so small coins need to sit close.
4. Light the coin from one side. A mirror-bright spot hides pits and splits. Turn the lens ring until the focus bar climbs. If the focus slider on the camera does nothing, the lens is manual.
5. Read the marks. "Look" means the mark is plain. "Maybe" means confirm it by eye.
6. For a split, use the light test. Shot A, move the lamp about a quarter turn without rotating the coin, shot B. A shadow moves with the lamp. A doubled die stays on the same letters.

Calibrate once on a coin you know, with that series selected. Later coins then get a millimeter reading, and a coin that does not match the selected series is flagged.

Space freezes the frame. Sample shows a marked practice coin with the camera left alone.

## What it will not do

A USB 2.0 camera will miss fine doubled dies, full bands, full bell lines, and most overdates. Those need a loupe. The edge lettering on presidential and Native American dollars is invisible in a face-on view. Tip the coin for the edge.

A rim bite can be mint-made or a later knock. The overlay says which clock position to inspect. It does not say the coin is valuable.
